## 2. The 5-Act Scriptwriting Architecture

Every Short follows an identical, tightly engineered **5-Act Narrative Structure** with zero wasted words, no intro greetings, and no "subscribe" begging:

### Act I: The Second-Person Premise + "Congratulations" Hook (0.0s – 8.0s)
- Immediately casts the viewer (`"You"`) as the operator of a curious, slightly shady, or paradoxical business.
- Drops a specific dollar figure within the first 5 seconds (`$50,000`, `$200 cheaper`, `$100,000 in cash`).
- Uses the channel's signature catchphrase: **"Congratulations."** followed by a twist:
  - *Video 1:* `"If you run a secret casino in Timmy's basement, congratulations. You're in the gambling business, the illegal kind. By Friday, you have a shoebox with $50,000 in it, but you can't spend it."`
  - *Video 2:* `"If you open a tiny phone shop right next to the big phone store and sell the same phone $200 cheaper, congratulations, you're in the phone business. But how are you cheaper than the company that makes it?"`
  - *Video 3:* `"You just made a hundred thousand dollars in cash in a gym bag from a job we're not going to talk about. Congratulations. You can't spend a cent of it."`

### Act II: The Friction / Why the Obvious Move Fails (8.0s – 18.0s)
- Explains the immediate roadblock in concrete, visual terms rather than abstract economics:
  - Buying a Lamborghini for `$50,000` makes both your friends and the tax inspector suspicious (`Video 1`).
  - Walking into Tony's car dealership with a gym bag of `$20` bills gets the police called because `"I found it is not a business plan"` (`Video 3`).
  - Sourcing phones locally makes it impossible to beat the manufacturer's store (`Video 2`).

### Act III: The Step-by-Step Mechanism & Real Business Terminology (18.0s – 45.0s)
- Walks through the operation in 3–4 crisp steps (`Step one... Step two... Step three...` or chronological escalation).
- **Crucial Educational Hook:** After illustrating a step with a funny physical example, the narrator names the real-world financial/business term:
  - *"This is called **money laundering**."*
  - *"This is called **placement**."*
  - *"This is called **layering**."*
  - *"This is called **integration**."*
  - *"This is called a **gray import**."*
  - *"This is called a **loss leader**."*
- This gives the viewer a dopamine hit of "I just learned how the real world works" while keeping the tone playful and accessible to a 10-year-old.

### Act IV: The "Timmy" Comedic Escalation (43.0s – 57.0s)
- **Timmy** (the recurring gullible customer/accomplice) enters the scene to provide a comedic payoff:
  - In *Video 1*, Timmy washes actual socks for free, is named Director of a palm-tree island shell company (thinking he got a promotion), and ends up with 12 police cars outside his house because his 1-machine laundromat reported `$50,000/month`.
  - In *Video 2*, Timmy buys a `$0.50` plastic case for `$30`, a `$0.10` screen guard for `$25`, `$12/month` insurance, and trades his phone back for `$150` so the shop can resell it for `$400`.
  - In *Video 3*, Timmy actually drives his muddy car into the fake car wash front; three mobsters in suits stare at a garden hose like it's a snake, his car comes out dirtier than it went in, and Timmy happily pays `$8` and leaves a 5-star review (`"very relaxing"`).

### Act V: The Signature One-Liner Sign-Off (Last 3–5 Seconds)
- Closes with a punchy summary statement that loops seamlessly back to the beginning:
  - *"And that's basically how every phone shop works."*
  - *"And that's basically how every money laundering scheme works."*
  - *"The inspector does the math. So do the 12 police cars outside Timmy's house."*

## 3. Visual Style & 3D World-Building Bible

### A. Character Design ("Wobbly" Toy / Clay Aesthetic)
- **Proportions:** Chibi/Mii-style stylized 3D figures (~3 heads tall), spherical heads, cylindrical torsos, stubby limbs, and **rounded mitten/pill hands with no individual fingers**.
- **Shaders & Materials:** Soft matte plastic/clay shaders for skin and props; subtle fabric weave normal map on suits and Timmy's striped shirt; warm global illumination with soft contact shadows.
- **Facial Animation:** 2D flat vector-style eyes, eyebrows, rosy circular cheek blushes, and mouth shapes projected onto the smooth 3D head sphere (similar to *Animal Crossing* or Lego/Mii facial rigs). Eyes swap quickly between neutral dots, happy upward crescents (`^ ^`), skeptical half-lids, and shocked `O` mouths.
- **Recurring Cast:**
  1. **The Protagonist ("You"):** Grey business suit, white shirt, red or dark tie, grey/taupe sculpted hair.
  2. **Timmy:** Bright orange curly hair, thick round black eyeglasses, freckles, yellow-and-navy horizontal striped polo shirt with white collar, blue pants.
  3. **Tony:** Burgundy tracksuit or dealer outfit, slicked dark hair, yellow-tinted aviator glasses, thin mustache, gold chain/nameplate (`TONY`).
  4. **The Government / Tax Man:** Grey bowler hat, round spectacles, grey mustache, yellow bow tie, grey suit.

### B. Environment & Prop Design (Diegetic Big-Text Labels)
- **Minimalist Toy Town:** Clean suburban streets, blue sky with soft clouds, simple pastel buildings (`ORBIT`, `WOBBLY PHONES`, `SPARKLE WASH`), wooden desks, and glass retail counters.
- **Diegetic Text Rule:** Every key number or noun spoken in the voiceover is simultaneously printed in **huge, bold, high-contrast uppercase text on a physical prop in the 3D scene**:
  - A-frame sidewalk signs: `ORBIT 9 $999` vs `ORBIT 9 $799`
  - Cash register screen: `ORBIT 9 $799` -> flips to `PROFIT $5`
  - Countertop product cards: `CASE - COST 50¢` next to tent card `$30`; `SCREEN GUARD - COST 10¢` next to `$25`
  - Contracts & clipboards: `INSURANCE - $12/MONTH - SIGN HERE` with a red `SEAL` stamp
  - Ledger book: `TUE: washed 2,010 | WED: washed 1,995 | THU: washed 2,004 | FRI: washed 2,000`
  - Whiteboard: `CUSTOMERS THIS MONTH: 0` and `INCOME THIS MONTH: $50,000`
  - Funny literal props: A blue mailbox labeled `TAXES`; a place card on a stack of cash reading `DENTIST - Tue 3:00`; a document stamped `MORTGAGE - APPROVED`; a yacht named `CAR WASH`.

### C. Camera Work & Editing Rhythm
- **Shot Duration:** 1.4 to 2.2 seconds per cut (roughly 28–35 distinct camera shots per 60-second Short).
- **Camera Motion:** Almost every shot has a continuous, smooth linear camera move—either a **slow dolly push-in**, a **horizontal truck/pan** across a table of props (e.g., panning from `$999` sign to `$799` sign, or panning from `COST 50¢` card to `$30` price tag), or an **over-the-shoulder reveal**.
- **Visual Literalism / Sight Gags:**
  - When saying *"you can't spend a cent of it"*, a giant yellow padlock drops onto the gym bag with a metallic clank.
  - When saying *"wipe it"*, the character literally rubs a yellow kitchen sponge across the smartphone screen.
  - When saying *"spent more on plastic than you made on the phone"*, a brass justice scale tips heavily toward the plastic case/screen protector over the phone box.

## 4. Typography & Subtitles Specification

- **Mode:** Single-word active display (1 word on screen at any instant, synced tightly to word onset).
- **Position:** Center-horizontal, slightly below vertical center (~55–60% from top of frame) so it sits right over the character's chest/desk without covering faces or top store signs.
- **Font Style:** Heavy rounded sans-serif, Title Case (e.g., `If`, `You`, `Open`, `Phone`, `Shop`), white fill (`#FFFFFF`) with a thick black stroke (`#000000`).
- **Dynamic Highlighting:** Financial figures (`$100,000`, `$200`, `$799`, `$650`, `$0.50`, `$30`, `$25`, `$12`, `$150`, `$400`, `$50,000`, `$8`) and high-impact words (`Cash`, `Gym`, `Cheaper`, `Charger`) pop in **Golden Yellow (`#FACC15` / `#FFD700`)** with a subtle scale-bounce animation.

## 5. Audio, Voiceover & Sound Design Blueprint

- **Voiceover Profile:** Crisp, warm, energetic American male narrator. Delivery is conversational, slightly conspiratorial, and deadpan-humorous—like a savvy older brother explaining how a hustle works.
- **Speech Cadence:** ~180–195 words per minute (3.0–3.25 words/sec). Zero long pauses (>0.4s); sentences are trimmed tightly in the DAW so each thought flows immediately into the next.
- **Background Music:** Low-volume (-22dB to -26dB under voice), quirky pizzicato strings / sneaking marimba / light acoustic guitar groove (sneaky "heist/capers" vibe for crime/laundering topics; upbeat quirky toy-shop groove for retail topics).
- **Layered Sound Effects (SFX every 1.5–2 seconds):**
  - **Cash/Money:** Crisp paper bill rustle, classic `cha-ching` register bell, coin clink, sparkle chime (`twinkle` SFX accompanied by 2D star particles on screen).
  - **UI/Text Reveals:** Soft pop/whoosh when signs appear, marker squeak when writing in the ledger or sticking the `0` on the whiteboard.
  - **Physical Actions:** Zipper zip (`gym bag`), heavy padlock clank (`can't spend a cent`), rubber stamp thud (`SEAL` / `APPROVED`), car horn/engine purr, water splash, phone notification ding (`5-star review`).
